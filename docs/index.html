What MacClip does

Your Mac normally remembers only the last thing you copied. MacClip quietly remembers your last 20 copies (text, formatted text and pictures) and lets you bring any of them back in one step. Just press Option + V.

Always thereLives in the menu bar, top right. No Dock icon, nothing in your way.
Starts by itselfOpens automatically when you log in.
PrivateEverything stays on your Mac. Nothing is ever sent anywhere.

Install

  1. Download MacClip (the .dmg file).
  2. Double-click the downloaded file to open it.
  3. Drag MacClip onto the Applications folder. Or double-click MacClip and choose “Move to Applications”.
  4. Open MacClip from your Applications folder.

If macOS says the app “cannot be opened”

MacClip is a small independent app, so macOS shows an extra warning the first time. You only do this once:

  1. Try to open MacClip. When the warning appears, click Done (or OK).
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll down to the message about MacClip and click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then open MacClip again.

First-time setup

The first time MacClip opens, it asks for one permission: Accessibility.

  • Why: it lets MacClip paste for you, so your choice appears right where you were typing.
  • How: click Continue, then switch MacClip on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.
  • Prefer not to? MacClip still works. Your choice is copied and you press ⌘ + V yourself.

MacClip never reads what you type and never sends anything anywhere.

How to use it

  1. Copy as usual with ⌘ + C. MacClip remembers each item.
  2. Open your history with Option + V anywhere, or click the MacClip icon in the menu bar.
  3. Pick an item. It appears where your cursor was, in the app you were using.

The menu-bar icon

Right-click (or Control-click) the icon for options:

  • Pause capture / Resume capture: stop remembering for a while.
  • Clear history: delete everything MacClip has saved.
  • Launch at login: turn automatic start on or off.
  • Accessibility: shows whether automatic pasting is on.
  • Quit MacClip

Good to know

  • MacClip keeps the 20 most recent items. Copying something you already copied just moves it back to the top.
  • It remembers items even after you restart your Mac.
  • Passwords are protected. Items that password managers mark as secret are never saved, and copies made inside 1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane and Keychain Access are ignored.
  • Very large items (over 1 MB of text or 25 MB of images) are skipped.
  • About to copy something sensitive from another app? Choose Pause capture first.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
Picking an item copies it but doesn’t pasteTurn on MacClip in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Until then, press ⌘ + V.
Pasting stopped working after an updateIn the Accessibility list, remove MacClip (the – button), then add it again with +.
Option + V types “√” or does nothingAnother app is using that shortcut. Quit it, or open MacClip from its menu-bar icon instead.
I can’t paste into a password boxSome secure fields block automatic pasting. The item is already copied, so press ⌘ + V.
A password I copied is in the listNot every app marks passwords as secret. Use Clear history, and Pause capture before copying secrets.
I can’t see the iconYour menu bar may be full. Close or hide some menu-bar apps.
Files copied in Finder aren’t listedMacClip remembers text and pictures only.

Uninstall

  1. Right-click the MacClip icon and choose Quit MacClip.
  2. Drag MacClip from the Applications folder to the Trash.
  3. Optional: delete the MacClip folder inside ~/Library/Application Support to remove saved history.
